### [H1] Strategic Report — Chief Executive's Review (p. 3)

FY2026 was a year of transition for Halden. Revenue was £86.4 million (2025: £93.0 million), a decrease of 7.0%, as destocking by automotive and general engineering customers continued through most of the year. Gross margin was 20.4% (2025: 23.0%), reflecting price competition and the higher cost of US dollar-denominated purchases. As a result, we reported an operating loss of £0.9 million (2025: operating profit of £3.5 million).

Despite these conditions, we strengthened our relationship with our largest customer, a UK manufacturer of automotive components, whose purchases from us grew by 20% during the year. We have taken decisive action on costs and working capital, and we enter FY2027 with a clear plan to return to profitability.

### [H3] Financial Review — Cash Flow, Working Capital and Financing (p. 5)

Net cash generated from operating activities was £1.9 million (2025: £4.6 million), of which working capital initiatives contributed £4.4 million. In the second half of the year we agreed revised terms with several of our principal suppliers in Korea, Japan and Taiwan, extending standard payment terms from 60 to 90 days from the bill of lading date and replacing documentary letters of credit with open account terms for suppliers representing approximately 35% of our purchases. Creditor days increased to 88 (2025: 62).

Trade receivables increased to £18.7 million (2025: £14.9 million), mainly reflecting the move of our largest customer to 90-day payment terms from January 2026 and the timing of shipments in June. Debtor days were 79 (2025: 59).

The Company is financed by a revolving credit facility provided by Wexcombe Bank plc. At the 31 March 2026 quarterly test date the Company did not meet its leverage covenant. On 12 May 2026 the bank agreed to waive the covenant breach for the March and June 2026 test dates; the conditions of the waiver are set out in note 15. Net debt excluding lease liabilities was £7.2 million (2025: £6.1 million).

### [H4] Principal Risks and Uncertainties (p. 7)

**Customer concentration.** Our largest customer accounted for 31% of revenue (2025: 24%) and our three largest customers for 52% (2025: 44%). The loss of, or a significant reduction in orders from, any of these customers would have a material effect on our results.

**Supplier relationships and terms.** We rely on a small number of manufacturers in Asia for around 60% of our purchases. Changes to the payment terms or to the availability of credit offered by our suppliers could affect our liquidity.

### [H8] Notes to the Financial Statements (extracts) (p. 21–34)

**2. Basis of preparation — going concern (p. 21).** The directors have prepared cash flow forecasts for the period to 30 September 2027. The forecasts assume that the revolving credit facility, which expires on 31 March 2027, is renewed on similar terms, that the Company meets the minimum EBITDA covenant at 31 December 2026 and that current supplier payment terms are maintained. In the base case the Company operates within its facilities, with minimum headroom of £0.9 million in November 2026. In a reasonably possible downside scenario, in which revenue is 5% below the base case, the Company would not meet the minimum EBITDA covenant at 31 December 2026 and would require a further waiver from the bank. The directors have taken into account the subscription for new shares of £4.0 million completed on 5 September 2026 (note 24). Renewal terms for the facility had not been agreed at the date of approval of these financial statements. These conditions indicate the existence of a material uncertainty which may cast significant doubt on the Company's ability to continue as a going concern. The directors nevertheless have a reasonable expectation that the facility will be renewed and have therefore prepared the financial statements on a going concern basis.

**13. Trade receivables (p. 28).** Trade receivables are stated net of a loss allowance of £0.4 million (2025: £0.3 million). Trade receivables more than 60 days past due were £2.6 million (2025: £0.9 million). At 30 June 2026, 91% of gross trade receivables (2025: 89%) were covered by trade credit insurance with Arcbridge Credit Insurance Ltd. No claims were made under the policy during the year, and impairment losses recognised on trade receivables were £0.1 million (2025: £0.1 million).

**15. Borrowings (p. 30).** The revolving credit facility is provided by Wexcombe Bank plc and is secured by a fixed and floating charge over all of the Company's assets. The Company did not meet the leverage covenant (net debt to EBITDA of not more than 3.0 times) at the 31 March 2026 test date. On 12 May 2026 the bank waived the breach in respect of the 31 March and 30 June 2026 test dates. As conditions of the waiver: (i) the facility limit was reduced from £15.0 million to £11.0 million; (ii) the margin increased from 2.25% to 3.75% over SONIA; (iii) until the facility is refinanced, the leverage covenant is replaced by a minimum EBITDA covenant of £2.5 million for the twelve months to 31 December 2026; and (iv) no dividends may be paid until the facility is refinanced. The facility expires on 31 March 2027. Discussions on a renewal have commenced, but no terms had been agreed at the date of approval of these financial statements. Accordingly, the amount drawn of £8.4 million is presented within current liabilities (2025: £9.0 million, within non-current liabilities).

**16. Trade and other payables — supplier finance arrangement (p. 31).** Trade payables include £7.6 million (2025: £3.9 million) of invoices confirmed under a supplier finance programme provided by Wexcombe Bank plc, under which participating suppliers may receive early payment from the bank. The Company pays the bank on the original due date of each invoice, which for most participating suppliers is 90 days from the bill of lading date (2025: 60 days). Following its annual review, the bank reduced the programme limit from £10.0 million to £3.0 million with effect from 1 August 2026. Certain suppliers agreed to extended payment terms on the basis that early payment would be available under the programme.

**24. Events after the reporting period (p. 34).** On 5 September 2026, Altmoor Holdings Ltd, the Company's parent company, subscribed £4.0 million in cash for 4,000,000 new ordinary shares of £1 each. The proceeds were received on that date and will be used for working capital.
